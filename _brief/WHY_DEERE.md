## Where it goes (my call)

- **Not on the seven cards.** Each card makes one claim about Deere's numbers, and a finance exec is following that argument card to card. A personal story in the middle of it breaks the thread.

> **Why Deere?** In my last year of high school, my uncle bet me $15 I wouldn't wear an ugly camo John Deere shirt to school. I wore it every Tuesday. Eventually over forty people at school, a few teachers included, cleared out the local Walmart's stock of John Deere shirts. The company has held a special place for me ever since, so it was the obvious one to take apart.

## Long version (transmission)

Working title: **John Deere Tuesdays**

In April of my last year of high school, I was visiting my uncle in Chicago. He saw a camo John Deere shirt that we both agreed was ugly, and he bet me $15 I wouldn't wear it to school. I've always been someone unrestricted by social conventions, so the kid who wore hot pink Air Force 1s was naturally unfazed. I started wearing the shirt every Tuesday to prove I could wear anything in my rotation as long as I had the confidence.

During the Orillia senior camping trip, five of my close friends surprised me by wearing the same shirt after they took an impromptu detour. Soon it became a running joke, extending to all sixteen members of our friend group. Eventually our influence spread, and over forty people at school, a few teachers included, sold out the local Walmart's stock of John Deere shirts.

As the original "John Deere pioneer," I looked forward to Tuesdays most. That group gave me the gall to speak to people I had never been able to approach before. I expanded my circle tenfold and learned that community can often be built on the margins of the mundane.

I still get nostalgic every time I see a green tractor. So when I wanted to build a real equity research report on a real company, I didn't have to think very hard about which one. [Here it is.](LINK)

## What I changed, and why

- **Cut the opening paragraph** ("bittersweet... change your mindset forever"). It's an admissions warm-up, and the story starts at Chicago anyway.
- **Cut the leadership and "cultural catalyst" paragraphs**, including "Any person... any study". That's Cornell's motto, written for Cornell's readers. "Flourish", "safe space" and "the machine of community" are application voice, not transmission voice.
- **Kept your sentences** for the bet, the Air Force 1s, Orillia, the sixteen friends, the Walmart and "the gall". "Gall" is already in "wanting things".
- **Changed "Last April"** to "In April of my last year of high school", because "last April" no longer points at the right year.
- **Small grammar fixes.** "He had bet me" became "he bet me". "I never had been able" became "I had never been able". The teachers moved next to the forty people they belong to.
- **New last paragraph** connects the shirt to the report. It says only what you told me: the company matters to you, and that's why you picked it.
- **Your site:** a transmission also needs an entry in `src/data/posts.ts`, or the feed, archive and OG card won't see it. Pushing to `main` there deploys live, so I haven't touched that repo.
